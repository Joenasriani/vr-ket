## Research question

What similarities and differences are defensible when comparing **VR immersion/presence** with **ketamine-associated dissociative experiences**, and what evidence exists for using VR during ketamine or esketamine treatment?

## Evidence boundary

The strongest defensible conclusion is more specific than the earlier version of this note:

- VR can alter presence, embodiment, attention and perception through mediated sensory input and interaction.
- Ketamine is an NMDA-receptor antagonist whose current U.S. injection labeling is for anesthesia. Ketamine is also studied and used off-label in some psychiatric settings. Intranasal esketamine has separate approved U.S. psychiatric indications.
- VR immersion and ketamine-associated dissociation can share high-level descriptions such as altered self/world perception, but they arise through different mechanisms and should **not** be presented as equivalent or interchangeable states.
- **Direct combined-use evidence exists, but it remains limited and heterogeneous.** It includes a published two-patient burn-wound case report, small completed registered studies, and current trials testing VR during ketamine/esketamine treatment.
- The existence of these studies does **not** establish that VR reliably potentiates ketamine, reduces adverse effects, improves antidepressant efficacy, or reproduces ketamine-associated dissociation.

## Direct combined-use evidence

### Published clinical evidence

**Maani et al. (2011)** reported two severe-burn patients who received ketamine during wound care and were compared within-session with and without immersive VR. Both patients reported lower pain ratings during the ketamine + VR condition. This is direct combined-use evidence, but it is a two-patient case report and cannot establish general efficacy or synergy.

- PMID 21481162: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21481162/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1526-4637.2011.01091.x

### Completed registered studies

- **NCT01889537 — Low Dose Ketamine VR Analgesia During Burn Care Procedure.** Completed Phase 2 study; registry enrollment was 3 participants. The registry does not provide posted efficacy results. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01889537
- **NCT06139016 — Using Virtual Reality to Control the Audio-visual Inputs During Ketamine/Esketamine Treatment.** Yale University; completed; actual enrollment 7. The registry reports posted results and measures tolerability, safety, treatment experience, altered-state measures and dissociation. No linked peer-reviewed publication was listed in the registry at the September 17, 2026 audit. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06139016

### Ongoing registered studies

- **NCT06781697 — TREASURE.** Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre; recruiting pilot randomized trial in treatment-resistant depression comparing concurrent IV ketamine + immersive VR with ketamine alone. Estimated enrollment: 31. Primary outcomes are feasibility; secondary outcomes include depression, anxiety, dissociation, cognition, EEG and VR tolerability. No results are posted. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06781697
- **NCT07422519 — VR-based mindfulness + esketamine.** Recruiting randomized study in treatment-resistant depression comparing VR-based mindfulness plus treatment as usual with treatment as usual alone; esketamine is part of the treatment-as-usual pathway. No results are posted. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07422519

Registered-study status describes what researchers planned or conducted; it does not by itself establish efficacy, safety, or clinical usefulness.

## What the evidence does not yet establish

- VR and ketamine do not have an established state-equivalence relationship.
- There is no mature evidence base showing that VR is a substitute for ketamine or reliably reproduces ketamine-associated dissociation.
- The evidence reviewed here does not establish a general antidepressant synergy between VR and ketamine/esketamine.
- The completed Yale study and ongoing trials show that the question is being tested; they do not justify treating the answer as settled.
- No universal response-rate figure for a combined VR-ketamine intervention is justified from the evidence reviewed here.
